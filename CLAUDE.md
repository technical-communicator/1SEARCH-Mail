### Starter site (what exists today)

The owner asked for a simple interim starter site and set the brief's build phases aside for now. It is plain HTML, CSS and JS with **no build step**, served by GitHub Pages from `main` at the repo root (`.nojekyll` turns off Jekyll processing).

- The header logo is the **coloured** mascot head (`images/mascot-head-color.webp`, cut out of the mascot art with a transparent background) with the name typed in Courier Prime. The owner prefers it over both the white line-art head and the cut-out-letter wordmark. `images/mascot-head-color-large.webp` is the bigger copy used in the home hero.
- **Home page** (`index.html`): the hero exists to get people to the main features fast. It has a short masthead, then a **carousel of three slides** (Events, Mail Club, Blog). It's a sideways scroll-snap row that auto-advances every 6s (`SECONDS_PER_SLIDE`). It pauses on hover, touch, focus or the Pause button, starts paused for reduced motion, and has dot buttons. On phones the slides are 88% wide so the next one peeks in. Below it, "Today in Orlando" reads `events.json` and shows up to 4 of today's events; the script also writes today's count onto slide 1.
- **Footer**: a dark footer that's identical on every page. **Phones get a compact version**: the logo, wrapped rows of links (headings visually hidden; the mail club column, blurb and note hidden) and a slim bottom bar. **Wider screens get the full version**: brand and blurb, Explore / Mail club / Say hi columns, and a bottom bar with © and Back to top (`#top` is on the header). Like the nav, it's duplicated in the four hand-written pages and in `footer()` in the build script. Keep them in sync.
- **Design system** (the owner asked for the blog's look across the whole site; `style.css` is organized into 14 numbered sections, with a contents list at the top): plain paper background (`--paper`, no full-page halftone); Anton for headlines and Courier Prime (typewriter) for all body text, labels and buttons; Yellowtail script only for small accents such as the signature, the marquee and "hi friend!". Use thin rules instead of heavy boxes and shadows. Every page opens with a `.masthead` (a `.section-chip` label, a giant `h1` and `.masthead-intro`), and later sections start with `h2.section-head`. Each section has a colour set by a `<body>` class: `page-events` blue, `page-mailclub` pink, `page-about` yellow, and blog/home red (the default). This sets `--section` and `--section-text`. Cards (`.post-grid`/`.post-card`, used on home, blog and listings) have an image, an Anton title with a `.card-arrow`, a dashed-rule summary and a meta line; the whole card is clickable. Halftone dots and cut-and-paste touches (event card bands, the polka-dot envelope, washi `.taped` strips) are accents only. Brand artwork lives in `images/` as resized web copies of the owner's files.
- `index.html`, `events.html`, `mailclub.html`, `about.html` (hand-written) and the generated blog pages share one `style.css` and repeat the same header, nav and footer markup by hand. If you change the nav, change it in all four files.
- `mailclub.html` is the SEARCH snail mail club page. Its copy comes from the owners (Halley and Zach) and is deliberately lowercase; keep that voice. The owner found the page "too much", so it now **opens with the pink mail club banner** (`banner.js`, `data-heading="1"` makes its headline the page's h1) instead of a masthead, and the banner is not repeated at the bottom. Order: banner, choose your subscription (envelope **tabs**, `role=tablist`, with `?tier=sticker|print|curator` to pre-select one), what's inside (polka-dot envelope + collage), how it works, artists and perks, the founders' letter, past mailers (`#past-mailers`) and the FAQ (`<details>`). Every Join button has `data-patreon`, and its address comes from the single `PATREON_URL` constant in the page's script. That constant is still a placeholder until the owner sends the real Patreon URL.
- Mail club **past features**: `mailers.json` lists months, newest first, each with an `artist` and a `business` (`name`, `about`, `image`, `instagram`, `post`). Months with `"placeholder": true` show grey "image coming soon" boxes until the owner sends the details. The page's script builds month chips and an artist/business pair. The blog's Monthly Mail recaps list (the `PAST-MAILERS` markers) sits under it. The "$450 given" stat is a quiet `.stat-line`, toned down at the owner's request.
- The mail club "What's inside?" collage is drawn **to real scale**: `--in` is 1/13 of the collage width. The letter is 6×11 in, the print and perk card are 4×6 in, the stickers are 2.5 in at their longest side, and the envelope is a #10 (9.5×4.125 in). Keep new items to scale the same way.
- **Mail club banner** (`banner.js`): a full-width, Shopify-style promo near the bottom of every page, outside `<main>`. It has a black ticker, a pink halftone banner (headline, black Join button, mascot print, "this month's artist" card) and three tier tiles linking to `mailclub.html?tier=…#tiers`. Pages include `<div data-mailclub-banner data-root="…"></div>` plus `<script src="…banner.js">`, where `data-root` is `""`, `"../"` or `"../../"`. The build script's `footer()` adds it to blog pages. **Each month, update `BANNER_SETTINGS` in `banner.js`** (month, artist + post, print and sticker images, perk + post + image) and swap the pictures in `images/mailer/` that the mail club collage uses. It replaced the per-page single mail club buttons and the blog's end-of-post box. Styles are in section 15 of `style.css`.
- `events.html` is laid out like a cinema showtimes page. Its inline script `fetch`es `events.json` and builds a 7-day strip (today plus the next six days). "Today" is computed in `America/New_York`, and dates are handled as `YYYY-MM-DD` strings at noon UTC so DST can't shift them. The chosen day is kept in `?day=YYYY-MM-DD` via `history.replaceState`. Cards are built with `textContent`. The card title is a `<button>` whose `::after` stretches over the whole card, and it opens a `<dialog>` with the enlarged card. Each event's `category` becomes a CSS class (`"Food and Drink"` → `.cat-food-and-drink`) that sets `--cat-colour` in `style.css`. Events outside the 7-day window are not shown. Below the cards is an **interactive map** (Leaflet 1.9.4, vendored in `vendor/leaflet/`, with keyless OpenStreetMap tiles faded with tile opacity; after 4 failed tiles it switches to Esri World_Light_Gray_Base, also keyless. CARTO was dropped because it started requiring an API key). It has tag-shaped pins in each category's colour showing the start time, and it updates with the chosen day and filter (`updateMap()` is called from `showDay()`). Mouse hover shows the same `.event-card` over the map; tap, click or Enter pins it open (pointer events, plus a 500ms guard against ghost clicks); hovering a list card highlights its pin. Scroll-wheel zoom is off so the page scrolls normally. Under the map: `.map-list` chips (one per pinned event; tapping one calls `focusPin()`, which centres the pin and, on phones, nudges it above the card) and `.map-days` (the 7 days again; `showDayKeepingMapInPlace()` scrolls to cancel out the list's height change so the map stays put). Tiles are see-through (`opacity`) over a paper-coloured background. **Don't put a CSS `filter` on the tile pane**: Safari on iPhone can draw filtered tiles over the pins. Changing day only scrolls the top day strip sideways, never the page. A simple **category filter** (one chip per category, from `CATEGORIES` in the script) filters the cards and the day counts; it is kept in the URL as `&cat=`.
- The `events.json` fields are `title`, `date`, `start_time`, optional `end_time`, `venue`, `category`, `description`, optional `link`, and optional `lat`/`lng` (venue coordinates for the map; approximate, placed from the street address, so add them when adding events. Events without them are left off the map, and the map says how many). The link is the event's Instagram, ticket page or website. The card button label is chosen from the URL (Instagram / Tickets / Website); the link opens in a new tab and sits above the card's stretched button via `z-index`. The owner edits this file by hand or sends their weekly "events this week" Instagram slides for Claude to add. When adding events from slides, research each one for its own event page; if there is none, link the host's or venue's Instagram and tell the owner which events got venue links.
- The owner is learning from this code. Keep it simple and well commented.
- **Blog** (the owner chose "paste and I build": they send articles in chat and Claude publishes them):
  - Sources are `posts/<slug>.md`, Markdown with YAML front matter: `title`, `date`, `description`, `tags`, optional `series`, optional `cover` + `cover_alt`.
  - Run `python3 tools/build_blog.py` (needs `pip install markdown pyyaml`). It generates `blog/<slug>.html`, `blog.html` and one page per series in `blog/series/`. It also refreshes the list between the `PAST-MAILERS` markers in `mailclub.html` from posts with `series: Monthly Mail`.
  - Series are defined in the `SERIES` list at the top of the script, each with a name, file slug, colour and one-line intro. A post's `series` must be one of them. They are shown as the coloured tabs fixed to the bottom of every blog page.
  - Blog design (the model for the whole site): list pages show the newest post as a lead story, then a 1/2/3-column grid of cards. Post pages have a centred headline, a summary line, a byline rule, a hero image, a narrow reading column, a Share button and "More from the blog". Coloured series tabs are fixed to the bottom. Blog-only styles are in section 10 of `style.css`. Never hand-edit the generated files; change the `.md` or the script. The script fails on em dashes.
  - When the owner pastes an article, keep their wording and lowercase voice. You may add links, short `##` subheadings and a `<p class="event-callout">` for event details; tell them what you added. If the post mentions a dated event, offer to add it to `events.json`. Deleting a `.md` does not delete its old `blog/*.html`; remove that by hand.
  - The nav (Events, Mail Club, Blog, About) is duplicated in the four hand-written pages *and* in `header()` in the build script. Keep them in sync.
- To preview locally, run `python3 -m http.server` from the repo root. `fetch` does not work over `file://`.
- **Deploys** use GitHub's automatic "pages build and deployment" workflow (no workflow file in the repo). If a run fails at the deploy step with `No artifacts named "github-pages" were found` while the build step succeeded, that's a temporary GitHub glitch, not a code problem. Re-run the workflow (Actions tab → the failed run → Re-run all jobs) or push any new commit.

## Workflow rules from the brief

- Build in the seven phases listed at the end of the brief, in order: setup, events read side, submissions, reviewer dashboard, mail club, blog, home + SEO pass. **Stop after each phase for the owner to review.** Do not start the next phase without being asked.
- The owner is not a full-time developer. The README must explain Supabase setup, secrets, deploy, approving events and publishing a blog post in plain language.
- Respect the "Out of scope" list: no map UI (but store coordinates), event images, "SCF Pick" badge, store or multiple reviewers. (Category filters were on that list, but the owner later asked for a simple one, and the starter site has it.)

## Planned architecture

- **Astro, static output**, hosted on **GitHub Pages** and deployed with GitHub Actions. There is no server, so anything that needs a secret runs in **Supabase Edge Functions**:
  - `submit-event` verifies the Cloudflare Turnstile token, validates every field (the limits are in the brief's `events` table), and inserts the row with `status = 'pending'`.
  - `approve-event` is reviewer only. It geocodes the address with Geocodio, sets `status = 'approved'`, and triggers a rebuild through GitHub `repository_dispatch`.
- **The data flow is build-time.** The public site reads approved events from Supabase while it builds, and generates one static page per event at `/events/<date>-<slug>`. Content changes only after a rebuild. Rebuilds happen when an event is approved **and** on a daily cron shortly after midnight Eastern, so "Today" in the day strip stays correct. Keep both triggers in the deploy workflow.
- **Supabase RLS:** anonymous users have no direct table access. Submissions only go through `submit-event`. The build reads approved rows only, and the single reviewer account has full access. `submitter_email` must never reach any public page or build output.
- **Secrets** (Geocodio key, Turnstile secret, GitHub token) live only in Supabase secrets and GitHub Actions secrets, never in front-end code.
- **Blog** is a Markdown content collection (title, date, description, tags, series, cover image). Posts can embed event cards by slug. Event pages link back to the posts that feature them, so both directions need to resolve at build time.
- The **reviewer dashboard** is a client-side route protected by Supabase auth. Exclude it from the sitemap and robots, and mark it noindex.

## Conventions

- Eight fixed categories, each with its own accessible colour: Music, Art, Markets, Food and Drink, Film, Comedy, Community, Workshops. Define the palette once as design tokens and reuse it everywhere.
- Event cards are text only and must look the same on the events page, the submit form's live preview, the reviewer dashboard and blog embeds. Build them as one shared component.
- Day links use `/events?day=YYYY-MM-DD`. Dates are in Eastern time.
- Zine and grunge styling goes in headings, accents and backgrounds only. Body text and listings stay clean and readable.
- **Never use em dashes in site copy.**
- SEO: every page gets a unique title, meta description and Open Graph tags. JSON-LD uses `Event`, `BlogPosting`, `FAQPage` (mail club) and site-wide `Organization`.
- Placeholders are expected for content the owner will supply later: mascot and logo art, Patreon link and tiers, and FAQ answers.
